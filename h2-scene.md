# H2 – Scene

## a) Podcast – Darknet Diaries: Blackshades

**Podcast:** Darknet Diaries  
**Episode:** 182 – Blackshades  
**Host:** Jack Rhysider  
**Date:** 3 November 2026  
**Link:** https://darknetdiaries.com/episode/182/

### Summary

- BlackShades was a Remote Access Trojan (RAT) that allowed attackers to access computers remotely, steal information, record keystrokes, spy through webcams and lock files for ransom.
- The malware spread through social engineering, malicious links, pirated software and infected USB devices. Its *Spreader* feature could also use social media accounts to target the victim's friends.
- BlackShades was sold online as monitoring software for around $40, although its features could be used for criminal activities.
- The malware infected more than 500,000 computers. An international law enforcement operation involving the FBI resulted in 97 arrests.

### Personal Reflection

- The episode shows that human behaviour and social engineering can be just as important as technical vulnerabilities in cyberattacks.
- I think organizations should focus on software security, trusted download sources and employee awareness to reduce malware infections.

**My question:** How can companies prevent malicious software installations when employees receive links from people they already trust?

## b) Blog – Schneier on Security

**Article:** Bloatware in Aftermarket Auto Parts  
**Author:** Bruce Schneier  
**Date:** 30 October 2026  
**Link:** https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/10/bloatware-in-aftermarket-auto-parts.html/

### Summary

- The article describes an aftermarket Android-based car entertainment system containing several unexpected pre-installed applications.
- Some applications had access to functions such as location services and call recording, raising security and privacy concerns.
- One application reportedly used about one-third of the device's processing power even when idle.
- The owner used an AI tool to investigate the software, showing how AI can help identify potential security and privacy problems.

### Personal Reflection

- I see a connection with the BlackShades episode because both demonstrate risks from software that users do not fully understand or control.
- I believe manufacturers should provide clearer information about pre-installed software, permissions and data collection.

**Scientific article:** Yang et al. (2026), *From 5G to 6G: A Survey on Security, Privacy, and Standardization Pathways*.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3785467

### Connection to the Research

- In H1, I studied cybersecurity risks in 5G and future 6G networks, especially threats related to IoT, AI, privacy and unauthorized access.
- BlackShades illustrates malware, social engineering and unauthorized access. Schneier's article highlights security and privacy risks in connected devices. These issues relate to the security challenges discussed in Section 4 of the scientific article.
- As 5G-Advanced and future 6G networks connect more industrial and enterprise devices, endpoint and software security will become increasingly important.
- From my telecommunications perspective, securing the network infrastructure alone is not enough. End-to-end security must also include devices, applications and third-party suppliers.

**My question:** How should mobile network operators and enterprise customers share responsibility for end-to-end cybersecurity?
